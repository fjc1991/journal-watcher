# Changelog

Release notes: `.github/workflows/release.yml` reads the bullets under the heading of the version being released (`## 1.2.0`) and shows them in the app's "Update available" dialog.

## 1.2.13

What the AI costs, and whether you can trust what it wrote:

- **An answer that was cut off says so.** When a model stops at its length limit, the end of the answer is missing. Such an answer used to be saved as if it were complete: a paper's summary, a chapter summary, an Ask answer. Now a cut-off summary is not saved (the paper says the summary was cut off and can be tried again), and Ask, Explain, the briefing, an idea outline and a related-work draft show a note with **Ask again**. A cut-off answer is never kept among the saved answers, so asking again really asks again. Chapter summaries, the book overview and the briefing have twice the room, so a retry can finish.
- **You see what a question costs before you ask.** Under the reader's Ask box: "Next question: about $0.03 · reads the 12 closest passages", or what the whole paper costs when "Whole paper" is chosen. Beside **Generate** in the Summary tab: what the summary costs, or that you have it saved and it is free.
- **The monthly budget has no gaps.** A paid embedding model is counted and stops at the budget. When the budget is used up, answers you already have and a free model on this computer still work. Calls whose model has no known price are still allowed, but the budget message and Settings → Usage now say how many there were ("so the real total is higher"). Before a large job, the page asks first if its estimate is more than what is left of the month's budget. An empty budget, or 0, means no limit; the field now shows that.
- **Screening does not pay three times for an answer it cannot use.** An answer that is not a list of verdicts is asked for once more, not twice. One that was cut off is asked again in halves, and the verdicts that did come are kept. Stop sends nothing more. The estimate before a search rests on what your own earlier screenings cost per paper, once 50 papers are on record.
- **"Summarise N papers" gives a range** while it can only go by the size of the files ("between about $0.40 and $1.62"), and one figure after three summaries with that model. A summary you cancel is recorded at what the PDF really cost to read.
- **Whole-paper questions: keep the paper ready (a switch, off unless you turn it on).** Settings → AI. With Claude, the first question on a paper then costs about a quarter more on the paper's text, and each further question within 5 minutes about a tenth of it. Off, every question pays for the whole paper, as before.
- **The same paper on your second computer finds its saved answers**: they are known by the PDF's content, not by where the file is.
- To know: answers saved before this version for a PDF (summaries) and for whole-paper follow-up questions no longer match, so the first repeat of each is asked, and paid for, once more. Answers that were cut off and saved before this version cannot all be recognised; press "ask again" on one that ends mid-sentence.

## 1.2.12

Safer and sturdier:

- **Only this computer can read your library through the app.** The app has no password because it only answers on your own computer. Saving and spending were already refused for anything else; now reading is too: a web page that pretends to be the app's address, or another site's page asking the app for your papers, gets nothing.
- **A link in an AI answer cannot run code.** Summaries, answers and notes are shown as formatted text. A link in them now only leads to a web address, an e-mail address or a place in the app; a picture is only loaded from the app itself (a picture from another site would tell that site what you are reading). The words of a link that is refused stay.
- **When something goes wrong inside the app, it says so.** An unexpected error used to end as "Can't reach the dashboard", as if the app had closed. Now the page shows what happened and the app carries on.
- **The export folder works the same everywhere.** `~/Dropbox/Notes` used to work for a backup and for "Save to my export folder", but "export this folder as a directory" made a folder literally named `~` where the app was running. All three now use the same place, and a name without a leading `/` is taken from your home folder.
- **Costs are written one way.** An estimate reads "about $0.12" or "under 1 cent" everywhere; an amount on record reads "$0.12" (and up to four decimals below a cent).
- For releases: the offline checks run before a release is built, and a release is refused if the one before it was signed and the signing key has gone missing from the project.

## 1.2.11

Keeping your work safe:

- **A daily snapshot of everything that is yours.** Once a day, when the app starts, your notes, highlights, folders, tags, reading status, library index and settings (not the PDFs) are copied to this computer, outside the synced folder; the last 14 are kept. **Settings → AI files & backup → Daily snapshots** lists them, and **Restore** puts them back as they were (what you have now is kept first, so a restore can be undone).
- **Deleting can be undone for 30 days.** A deleted paper or book, with its notes and highlights, is kept for 30 days: press **Undo** right after deleting, or **Put back** under Settings → AI files & backup → Recently deleted. It returns to its folders with its reading status.
- **Notes are never overwritten when they cannot be read.** If a paper's notes file was only half synced or was damaged, the reader used to open the paper as if it had no highlights, and your next highlight replaced the file. Now it tells you, saves nothing over it, and keeps a copy.
- **Two computers: more than the library list can be joined.** When Dropbox keeps two copies of a paper's notes, your reading status, a folder, your tags or the AI spending record, **Join the two copies** (Settings → Computers & sync) keeps what is in either.
- **Downloads fail more clearly and more safely.** A PDF is written whole or not at all. One paper that cannot be saved no longer stops the batch; a full disk stops it and says so. If the sign-in browser cannot start, the run says that once, instead of every paper ending as "blocked". If the app is quit during a download, the run offers **Go on with the download that was cut off**.

## 1.2.10

- **Import the references of a document.** "Import references" (Library → Add a paper, or the command palette) now takes a document that cites papers, not only a .ris or .bib file: **Word**, **PowerPoint**, **PDF**, a text file or a list you paste (a syllabus, a reading list, a paper's bibliography). The references are found by how they are written, with no AI and nothing sent anywhere, then each is looked up on Crossref and checked against what your document prints: the title, and the author or the year. You see which ones you already have. A record that only resembles a reference is listed unticked ("not sure it is this one"), a whole book is not downloaded, and a reference Crossref does not know is listed for you to decide.
- **You choose where they go**: the library only, one of your folders, or a new folder named after the document. The papers you already have go into the folder at once, the others when they arrive. They are downloaded by the usual routes: free copies first, then your publisher sign-ins and your university library.
- **The AI as a backup.** If the list misses some, **Ask the AI** reads the document and lists its references too. The button shows what it costs first; what it lists is checked on Crossref like the rest, and a DOI it gives is used only if your document prints it.

Tick the right person (or two entries of the same person: OpenAlex sometimes lists someone twice after a move) and press **Download all into their folder**: every journal article, conference paper and working paper goes into a folder named after them, with no question asked and no screening; each paper gets a summary, as any download does. A working paper is left out when its published version is listed. Papers already in your library go into the folder instead of downloading again, and papers you deleted are not fetched again (nor the preprint of a published paper you deleted). Searching the same person again fills the same folder; another person of the same name gets "name · institution". If OpenAlex stops answering, the run says so and what it listed.

- Paper cards show the paper's own journal name (papers from journals you do not watch showed an abbreviation).
- **Follow an author.** On the Authors tab, **Follow** does what Download all does (every work into the author's folder), then each weekly check brings the works OpenAlex lists for them since into that folder: no screening, and no summary written by itself (the Library's “Summarise N papers…” does that, with its estimate first). A work the journal feeds bring too is the same paper, not a second copy. A work that could not be downloaded (a human check, no access) is tried again the next week; its human check is offered with **Open the check**, never opened by itself. **Your authors** lists the authors with a folder, each with a Follow box (untick it to stop; the folder stays). It first shows what it would do and cost, with nothing spent: the chapters and sections without a summary (those already summarised are not asked again), and one overview of the whole book. **Summarise** runs it in the background (the AI activity tray can cancel it), one part at a time, then writes the overview from those summaries only (never the book's text), shown at the top of the panel. A refused key, the monthly budget or three failures in a row stop it; while a part is missing no overview is written, and pressing the button again does only what is missing. An empty answer is no longer saved as a chapter's summary.
- **A backup model for screening.** When the model chosen for screening can't answer in the structured form screening needs (OpenRouter's scaledown/summarize, for one), the run is no longer left unscreened: the remaining papers are screened with the cheapest model from the same provider that can (the run says so when even that one costs more than twice yours). The run's page says which model screened and why, and leaves your setting alone; **Make it the screening model** switches with one click. The candidates come from OpenRouter's public model list (read once a day; nothing about you is sent) or, for Claude, any Claude model. A refused key, the budget or a too-long request stop the screening as before.
- **Human checks:** HBR and ACM pages saved as PDFs now offer **Open the check** too (their checks ended as "Needs a human check" with no way to pass them during the download). In a download of nothing but papers that need a check, the app no longer tells you to press **Open the check** seconds before it opens the check by itself.
- **The OpenAlex and Elsevier keys go to the Keychain**, as the AI keys do (Settings → Paper sources), instead of the `.env` file of the synced data folder. Keys already in the file are moved by **Move them to the Keychain** (Settings → Computers & sync), with the AI keys. The Unpaywall e-mail stays in the file.
- The app tells OpenAlex, Crossref, Unpaywall, Semantic Scholar and Open Library plainly who is asking (Journal Watcher, its version and its project page; no e-mail address) instead of a browser's name. Feeds and publisher pages are read as before.
- **Fixed: SSRN papers still did not download from SSRN (1.2.6).** SSRN's page also names the PDF in a hidden tag for indexing tools, an address without "&mirid=1" that SSRN sends back to the paper page; the app took that one over the **Download This Paper** button. It now takes the button's address, and opens it from the paper page, as your browser does when you click it.

## 1.2.5

- **Fixed: a weekly check could mark every paper "screening failed" and leave them so for good.** When the model chosen for screening cannot answer in the structured JSON that screening asks for (a summarising model, say, after “Use one for everything”), the provider refuses each request. The app asked again three times per batch, then marked the papers as failed without saying why, and a weekly check never looked at them again, because they were already seen. Now a refused request (a model without that ability, a key or account the provider refuses: HTTP 400, 401, 402, 403, 404, 422) stops the screening at the first batch. The papers stay unscreened instead of failing, and the run's page says why, in the provider's words, with what to check. A busy provider is still asked again; a batch that still fails, or an answer that is not the JSON asked for, marks only those papers, and each card says why.

- **The Test for “Screening new papers” checks that the model can screen.** Settings → AI → Which model does what: its Test sends one small screening request with the real schema, so a model that answers questions but cannot give verdicts is flagged there, not in a weekly check. The Test of “Use one for everything” does the same, since that model would screen too.
- **Summarise the papers that have no summary.** The Library has a “Summarise N papers…” button for every downloaded paper without an AI summary (books are left out, as always). It first says how many, which provider and model (Settings → AI → Paper summaries), and what it may cost and on what that guess rests: your earlier summaries with that model when there are at least three, else the price table, else “unknown”. Nothing is spent until you press “Write the summaries”. It then runs in the background, one paper at a time, in the AI activity tray (Cancel works there), without opening the download browser. A summary written meanwhile on another computer is not paid for twice. A refused key, the monthly budget or three failures in a row stop it, and the Library says what came of it.
- An empty answer is no longer saved as a paper's summary (it counted as summarised for good, so nothing asked again).
